CASPER / THE METHOD

THE CORE IDEA

Match score = Compatibility − λ × Conflict²

Reward common ground. Increase the cost of friction. Keep the reasoning visible.

01

Understand the people

Normalize five survey scores to 0–1 using the selected input scale. Keep shared interests as sets. Reject invalid and missing required values.

02

Score the connections

Cleanliness and sleep each contribute 30%, social energy 20%, and shared interests 20% through Jaccard similarity. If neither person lists interests, redistribute that weight to lifestyle.

03

Respect the limits

Snoring risk is the average of each person’s snoring level multiplied by the other’s sensitivity. Exclude edges above the conflict limit or with a non-positive adjusted score.

04

Pair, then refine

Take the highest scoring available edges. Explore both ways to swap two pairs, accepting only valid improvements. Stop after convergence or 10 passes.

HONEST ABOUT PERFORMANCE

Fast. Explainable. A heuristic.

Scoring is O(n²). Sorting candidate edges is O(n² log n). Refinement adds O(Pn²), where P ≤ 10. Storage is O(n²), assuming a fixed feature set.

This is not a proof of optimality, stability, fairness, or maximum matching size. Greedy selection can leave students unmatched even when another arrangement would pair more people.

The lowest pair score is visible in run details. There is no formal fairness objective in this version.

WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN

A score is a conversation starter.

A score of 85 means 0.85 on this model, not an 85% chance of a successful living arrangement. Settings and survey design affect the result.

The original survey anchors were not included in the repository. Confirm what low and high values mean before importing real responses, especially for sleep and snoring.

Use consented, appropriate cohorts. This demo does not enforce hostel eligibility, room capacity, mutual preferences, or safeguarding requirements.

THE QUESTION THAT STARTED IT

What if finding a roommate started with how we live?

Room allocation puts people together. Compatibility asks what sharing a room might actually feel like. CASPER explores that gap through everyday routines and explainable matching.

01 / EMPATHIZE

Listen to daily life.

The group project used form responses to learn about habits, shared interests, and sources of friction.

02 / DEFINE

Make tradeoffs visible.

Turn the broad idea of a “good roommate” into specific preferences and interpretable conflict signals.

03 / IDEATE & PROTOTYPE

Explore better pairings.

Combine compatibility scoring, pruning and local improvements into an interactive browser experience.

04 / TEST NEXT

Learn from people.

Validate survey anchors, gather consented feedback, compare against exact matching, and evaluate lived outcomes.

PROJECT LINEAGE

A group idea, a fresh implementation.

CASPER stands for Constraint-Aware Adaptive Score-based Pairing with Equilibrium Regulation, the name supplied with the group project. The name describes the project concept; this prototype does not establish mathematical equilibrium.

This edition is an independent web rebuild by ASHRITH RAJU, based on the supplied project notes and C++ prototype. The original Python project and survey schema are acknowledged below.
